01 / The idea

Separate facts, logic, and beliefs.

Suppose you own half a lemonade stand. Last summer it sold 1,000 cups. You expect some growth, charge $2.50 per cup, and spend about 40% of revenue on variable costs. Would charging $3.00 make the business more valuable?

Burr makes that question explicit in four kinds of files:

  1. LogicTemplates define how drivers combine.
  2. FactsCSV files hold reported observations.
  3. BeliefsParameters hold assumptions and scenarios.
  4. LearningA journal keeps questions and results.

We will build the stand, inspect its forecast, try premium pricing, record the result, and share an interactive model. All numbers in this starter are illustrative. Its valuation treats modeled profit as free cash flow and omits the complexity of a real business.

03 / Understand the calculation

Follow the drivers to the forecast.

The starter template contains three model lines. Business assumptions live in parameters, so the same logic can evaluate different beliefs.

Excerpt · templates/lemonade.yaml
lines:
  cups: grow(cups0, cup_growth)
  revenue: cups * price
  profit: revenue * (1 - cost_pct)

grow compounds annual growth from the base-year cup count. Growth falls from 10% in 2027 to 5% in 2029, passing through 7.5% in 2028. The cost assumption is a distribution with a mean of 40%; an ordinary forecast uses that mean.

04 / Ask a question

What if customers accept a higher price?

Create a patch proposing a premium scenario. The price carries a rationale and source alongside its value. For this teaching example, the source is explicitly hypothetical.

Terminal · creates premium.yaml in your workspace
cat > premium.yaml <<'YAML'
op: add_scenario
name: premium
thesis: Customers accept a higher price with unchanged volume growth and cost ratio.
bindings:
  price:
    value: 3.0
    rationale: A premium for fresh ingredients and a convenient location.
    source: hypothetical customer interviews for this tutorial
    confidence: medium
YAML

burr --json try stand premium.yaml

The result is conditional on the model: it contains no relationship between a higher price and lower demand. That becomes a useful next question for research or a richer template.

05 / Preserve the reasoning

Record the finding, then save the scenario.

Attach the question and interpretation to machine-computed results. On this fresh workspace, the first record is exp-001.

  --question 'What if customers accept a higher price?' \
  --finding 'A 20% price increase raises value 20% when volume and the cost ratio hold.' \
  --tags pricing
burr --json log stand --param price
burr --json replay stand exp-001
